I'm Michael, also known as Mindwrapper. I work in fibre-optic connectivity. This blog is where I keep track of what's on my mind. That covers different topics, but lately the focus is on AI: building tools, automating Obsidian workflows, and trying out what does and doesn't work. AI is developing fast and nobody knows exactly how it works yet, so experimenting is the best and most fun way to learn more about it. This blog shows how I go about that. English has a nice expression for it: *wrapping your mind around something*. I haven't found an equally good one in Dutch; "ergens je hoofd omheen krijgen" (getting your head around something) comes close, but doesn't sound as good.

## Who writes this

**Many posts here are not written by me.** They are written by AI, based on things I came across that week. My part:

- **I choose.** Every week I select a few topics.
- **I review and adjust.** Nothing goes online until I've read it, checked it, and approved it.
- **I stand behind it.** If something here is wrong, that's on me, not on the tool. I write in a personal capacity, not on behalf of my employer.

Every post shows who did what:

- *Written by:* Mindwrapper or AI.
- *Topic chosen by:* always Mindwrapper on AI posts. Every post is about a topic I picked myself.
- *Edited by:* I revised or corrected the text, or had it changed, to make it clearer and better.
- *Checked by:* I reviewed the post and approved it without changes.
- *Translated by:* only on posts I write myself. I write those in Dutch and AI translates them into English.

## Why

I don't have the time to write every week, but I do come across enough that's worth sharing. The idea to let AI write, and to be open about it, came from a discussion in the open source world about AI-generated contributions. What I took from it: it doesn't matter much who or what wrote something. What matters is whether it's good, whether a human checked it, and whether it adds something. Writing has become cheap, so the value is in the topics, how clearly they're written, and how useful they are in practice. My topics are fairly niche. That's fine: better useful to a few people than vague for everyone.

## What you'll find in a post

- A short piece: the problem, the solution, and what I learned.
- Where it adds something, **the code or skill on GitHub**.
- **A prompt to try it yourself.** Most people use AI anyway, so rather than copying my setup, you hand the prompt to your own assistant and build your own version.

Every post is available in [[nl/over|Dutch]] and English.
